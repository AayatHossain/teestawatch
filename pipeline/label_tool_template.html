Your job

Paint every pixel that is water on the radar date shown under the image. Everything you leave unpainted counts as land. Each pixel is 10 m; a tile is 2.56 km across.

Reading the radar

  • Calm water is very dark: black in "Radar grey", deep blue or black in the colour views. Rivers, ponds and flooded fields look like this.
  • Land is grey or bright: fields, sand bars, trees, villages. Villages and trees are often bright yellow or white spots.
  • Flooded fields with plants sticking out can look bright, not dark. If the optical image shows water there, paint it water.
  • Wind or rain can make water look rough and grey. Use Unsure if you cannot tell.
  • The grainy salt-and-pepper texture is normal radar noise; don't follow single dots. Paint the shapes. Radar smoothed (the default) removes most of it; switch to Radar raw to check thin channels and edges.
  • Purple-ish patches on land are usually wet or smooth fields, not open water. Open water is clearly darker (deep blue to black).

Helper layers

  • Optical and Optical water view (water = blue) are photos taken up to 3 days from the radar date, so water may have changed a little. White blobs are clouds; ignore them.
  • Background map is an older photo of a normal day (needs internet). Use it to see villages, roads and ponds, not the flood.
  • Height above stream: dark = low ground near streams. It is only a hint; trust the radar.

Rules

  • Do not use any other water map or AI tool. Your labels are the test answer key.
  • Keep Unsure small (ideally under 10 % of a tile). It is left out of scoring.
  • About 10–15 minutes per tile is normal. Zoom in for edges.
  • Your work is saved in this browser automatically. When finished, click Download my labels and send the file.

Keys

B water, U unsure, E eraser, F fill, [ ] brush size, 1–7 layers, H hide labels, Ctrl+Z/Ctrl+Y undo/redo, D done, N/P next/previous tile, mouse wheel + Ctrl to zoom.

Background map: Esri World Imagery (Esri, Maxar, Earthstar Geographics). Radar: Sentinel-1 (ESA) via NASA ASF HyP3. Optical: NASA HLS.
